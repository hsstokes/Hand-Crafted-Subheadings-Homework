⚡ Important: Choose Your Option First!

In class, you will use your letters in one of two ways. You need to decide now because it changes what letters you make.

🔤

Option A: Make a Font

Upload to Calligraphr → Get a font file

Your letters become a font you can type with. Calligraphr will change your letters into a font file. You can then type any word.

✓ Good for:
  • Clean, neat letters
  • Drawn or painted letters
  • When you want to type many words
📝 What letters to make:

Make A to Z (all 26 letters) — you need the full alphabet to type any word

✂️

Option B: Cut Out in Photoshop

Copy and paste each letter

You cut out each letter in Photoshop and put them together. This keeps all the texture and marks from stamps or collage.

✓ Good for:
  • Heavy texture and ink marks
  • Collage letters from magazines
  • Stamps with messy ink
📝 What letters to make:

Make only the letters you need for your title word(s) — for example, "TEXTURE" needs T, E, X, U, R

🤔 Which Should I Choose?

Do you want to keep all the texture, ink marks, and messy parts in your letters?

What To Do

1
Decide Your Option (A or B)

Look at the two options above and decide:

  • Option A (Font): You need to make all 26 letters (A to Z)
  • Option B (Photoshop): You only need to make the letters in your title word

Write down your choice! You will tell your teacher in class.

2
Choose Your Title Words (Option B only)

If you chose Option B, you need to decide on your subheading.

Your subheading should capture your area of research focus. It's not just a random word — it's the concept driving your research and making.

To find yours, ask yourself:

  • What's the theme connecting your artist research?
  • What idea or concept are you exploring?
  • If you had to explain your research focus in one phrase, what would it be?

Once you've decided:

  • Write down your chosen word or short phrase
  • Note which letters you'll need to create

Option A students: skip this step — you're making all 26 letters.

3
Get Your Template

Download a template from Calligraphr. This has boxes for your letters:

  • Go to Calligraphr.com
  • Log in with your account from Session 1
  • Click "Templates"
  • Option A: Choose all letters A-Z
  • Option B: Choose only the letters you need
  • Download as PDF
  • Print on white paper
Go to Calligraphr →
4
Get Your Materials

Get what you need for your chosen way:

🎨 What You Need

  • Letter stamps: Stamps + ink pad (black or colour)
  • Foam stamps: Foam sheets + scissors + glue + cardboard + ink or paint
  • Cut and stick: Old magazines + scissors + glue stick
  • Drawing: Pens, pencils, or markers
  • Painting: Brushes + paint (acrylic or watercolour)
5
Make Your Letters

Put one letter in each box on your template:

  • Keep each letter inside its box
  • Make letters with interesting texture
  • Mistakes are OK — they make it look handmade
  • Wait for ink or paint to dry completely
6
Scan Your Template

Make a digital copy of your finished template:

  • Use a scanner — choose 300 DPI (this means high quality)
  • Or use a phone app: Adobe Scan or Microsoft Lens
  • Save as JPEG or PNG
  • Check it is clear and straight
⚠️

Important: Stamps Look Backwards!

When you look at a stamp, the letter looks the wrong way. This is normal! When you press it on paper, it prints the right way.

  • If you make your own stamp, draw the letter backwards
  • Test on paper first before using your template
  • Press hard and flat for a good print
💡

Tips for Good Results

  • Texture is good: Uneven ink and brush marks look interesting
  • Dark on white: Dark letters on white paper scan best
  • Protect your table: Put old newspaper under your work
  • Make extras: Make 2 or 3 of each letter — then choose the best one
  • Wait to dry: Make sure everything is fully dry before you scan

💭 Think About Your Style

Before you start, think about how you want your letters to look:

  • Do you want bold and strong or soft and light?
  • Does your way match your project idea?
  • How will these letters look with your other portfolio work?
